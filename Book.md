Section 2 — "Humans Wondered: The Universe Before the Technology"

Status: ⚠️ Reads like a syllabus in the middle.

The Newton → Kepler → Galileo → Einstein stretch lists names and one-line achievements back to back. It's the one part of the book so far that a non-physics reader could skim past.

Ideas to plant here:

After "He imagined what it would be like to travel alongside a beam of light" — stop and hand that to the reader directly. Close your eyes right now. Imagine riding next to a beam of light. What do you think you'd see? Let them sit in their own confusion for a sentence before Einstein's answer ever shows up.
A stop-and-stare moment for the ancient-sky paragraph: put the reader standing in a field 3,000 years ago, zero light pollution, an unexplainable ceiling of light overhead with no science to explain it yet — what story would you have invented?
Possible image: a night sky over an ancient landscape, or a Babylonian star chart.
Consider slowing down instead of naming four scientists in four sentences — pick the single most vivid detail for each, or fold two of them into one image-driven paragraph.
